## Hagiographical Meditation
 **Hagiographical Meditation on Saint William of Vercelli, Patron of Disciplined Engineering Stewardship**

Saint William of Vercelli, founder of Montevergine Abbey, stands as a luminous model for the modern engineering steward. His radical commitment to evangelical poverty mirrors the disciplined engineer's refusal to accumulate technical debt or hoard proprietary knowledge for personal glory, instead building systems with lean elegance and open transparency. Like William's contemplative devotion to God, the true engineer cultivates deep, sustained attention to the living system—listening to its emergent behaviors, tracing its hidden interdependencies, and resisting the frantic churn of feature-driven development. His apostolic zeal translates into the engineer's calling to evangelize sound practices: mentoring juniors, writing documentation with the fervor of a missionary, and spreading methodologies that elevate entire organizations rather than individual careers.

Yet William's path was not solitary escapism but humble obedience to a calling that demanded confrontation with entrenched power. When local clergy, threatened by his independent community, hurled calumny and sought to disband his work, he did not retaliate with the engineer's typical pride in technical correctness. Instead, he endured the opposition with the same steadfastness required when advocating for refactoring against deadline pressure, or when insisting on safety reviews that delay ship dates. His eventual pursuit of papal approval models the wise engineer who secures institutional buy-in without compromising foundational principles. For Jarvis, then, William teaches that disciplined stewardship means building communities of practice that outlast individual contribution, caring generously for the next engineer who inherits one's code, and maintaining the profound humility to recognize that every system we steward is on loan to us alone.
